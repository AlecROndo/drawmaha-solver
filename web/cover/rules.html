The rules · one hand, in order

Omaha's board, a draw in the middle, one pot paid twice.

Drawmaha is a split-pot draw/Omaha hybrid. Five cards each, a shared board, one draw between the flop and the turn, and at showdown the same five cards are read twice: half the pot to the best Omaha hand, half to the best five-card draw hand. Nobody has solved it. That is what this site is for.

One hand, in order

Five cards, five streets, one draw, two ways to win.

Scroll, and the felt plays a hand along with you: the one this page follows, A♠ K♠ 7♥ 7♦ 3♣. Every street is followed by a betting round; the draw sits between the flop and the turn.

1Deal

Five cards, face down, to everyone.

You pick yours up; nobody else sees them. Five, not Omaha's four, because these five will be read twice at the end: you are already holding two hands. Ours is A♠ K♠ 7♥ 7♦ 3♣, two big spades, a pair of sevens, and a three along for the ride.

Then the first betting round, as one follows every street.

2Flop

Three cards to the board.

Face up, shared by everyone, like Omaha's flop. Your Omaha hand will use exactly two from your hand and exactly three from the board. Ours comes Q♠ J♠ 7♣: two more spades, a third seven, and two broadway cards that could turn into a straight.

flop 3 to the boardbet
3Draw

Discard up to five, take as many back.

The one street Omaha never gives you. Throw away any number of your five, from none to all, and take that many fresh off the deck, already knowing three fifths of the board. It is the only moment your hand changes, and it is where the game's size explodes: thirty-two ways to discard, each with its own future.

We give up the 3♣, the one card doing nothing, and draw the 10♠.

4Turn

A fourth card to the board.

From here the hand runs as Omaha does. The board is four, your Omaha hand still uses exactly three of them, so the combinations multiply. Ours is the 4♦, a blank.

5River

The fifth and last.

Five on the board, five in your hand, one betting round left. The river is the 9♠, and the board reads Q♠ J♠ 7♣ 4♦ 9♠.

river 1 to the boardbet
6Showdown

The same five cards, read twice. The pot, cut in two.

Half goes to the best Omaha hand: exactly two from the hand plus three from the board. Half goes to the best five-card draw hand: your five as they stand, no board. One holding can take both halves, or one, or neither.

If two hands tie for a half, that half is split again between them, so a pot can be quartered. A full board or a full hand is never required to tie: the same five cards, read the same way, tie.

Two ways to win

The same five cards: a straight flush one way, a pair of sevens the other.

At showdown nothing is added to your hand: the five you hold after the draw are the five that get read. They are read by two rules at once, and the two rules can disagree wildly about how good they are.

The Omaha hand takes exactly two of your five and exactly three of the board's five, whichever pair-plus-three makes the best poker hand. The draw hand is your five as they lie, with no help from the board at all.

Every bet on every street was made with both readings in mind. A holding that is right for one half and wrong for the other is the ordinary case, and it is why the game has never been solved.

Who we are

A solver library for a game with no solver.

Drawmaha Solver is the solver for Drawmaha, written from the ground up because none existed. The method is Deep CFR: counterfactual regret minimisation, with neural nets standing in for the regret tables once the tables no longer fit in memory.

Three rungs are climbed and live on this site. The fourth, mini-Drawmaha, is being built. The fifth is the full game.

What we stand for

Nothing climbs unchecked.

A solver that has never been checked against an answer is a guess with a progress bar. So each rung is a smaller game with a closed form or a published solution, and we publish the gap: exploitability in chips, and the game value beside the exact value.

When the gap closes, we climb. When it does not, we do not, and the ladder says so.

Why nobody has solved it

2.6 million holdings, 32 draws from each, two rankings on one pot.

Hold'em solvers exist because the game is small enough to enumerate and the answer is one number per spot. Drawmaha is bigger on every axis at once.

2,598,960C(52,5)
five-card holdings

Every hand starts from one of 2,598,960 five-card holdings, against 1,326 in hold'em and 270,725 in Omaha. The deal alone is bigger than the whole of a smaller game.

322⁵
discard patterns per holding

At the draw each of the five cards stays or goes: thirty-two patterns, each opening onto a fresh set of cards off the deck, all decided with the flop already showing.

2rankings
hands to hold at once

The pot pays two different readings of the same five cards, so a spot has no single strength. A strategy is right for one half and wrong for the other, and the solver has to weigh both.
