Benchmarks

Four games with known answers, and how far each solver is from them.

Each solver is graded by exploitability: the chips per hand a best response wins against its average strategy. Zero is a Nash equilibrium. Each game was solved and graded before the next was started.

Benchmark 1 · Rock-paper-scissors

Regret matching finds the uniform Nash of rock-paper-scissors

Two regret matchers play each other. Each keeps a ledger of how much better every throw would have done, and mixes in proportion to the positive part. The current strategy wanders; the average converges.

0.0009chips a round exploitable, after 100k rounds
+0.24a round against 50% rock, of the +0.25 a best response earns

Benchmark 2 · Kuhn poker

Vanilla CFR on Kuhn poker reaches 0.00063 of the closed-form equilibrium

Three cards, one bet, twelve information sets. The equilibrium is a one-parameter family known in closed form, and the game is small enough that all 64 pure strategies are enumerated for the grade. The game value is −1/18 for the first player.

Benchmark 3 · Leduc poker

Vanilla CFR on Leduc poker grades 0.011 against an OpenSpiel solve

Six cards, two rounds, one community card, 288 information sets. There is no closed form, so the reference is OpenSpiel's solve of the same game, frozen in the repository as a fixture.

Benchmark 4 · Mini-drawmaha

Linear CFR on mini-drawmaha grades 0.061 at 20 million hands per seat

6,220,050 information sets, sampled by lockstep MCCFR, four regret rules raced on one seed. The grader is an exact best-response walk; no outside engine plays this game.

Benchmark 5 · Deep CFR

Deep CFR on mini-drawmaha grades 0.086 at 2 million hands per seat

The same sampler and the same grader, with the regret table replaced by a 4 × 256 network per seat and its regret targets divided by √pot. Graded at equal hands per seat against the table.

Next · Full Drawmaha

Full Drawmaha has not been started

2,598,960 holdings, 32 discards from each, two rankings on one pot. The plan is Deep CFR at scale, graded by head-to-head checkpoint matches and a trained exploiter, since no exact walk crosses the full tree. A research project by Alec Ondo. A heads-up pot-limit Drawmaha solver, built one solved game at a time, each graded against a known answer. Python, NumPy and torch; training on Modal; the site on Vercel. The code, the graders and the data behind every figure are public.
