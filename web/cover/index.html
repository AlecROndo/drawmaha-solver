What we stand for

Known answers first

Every rung is a smaller game whose solution is already in the literature or in closed form. The solver has to reproduce it, to the third decimal, before anything moves up.

 

Exploitability, not vibes

A strategy is graded by how many chips a best response wins against it. That number is printed on every rung and it is the only score that counts.

 

The ledger is the product

Regret tables, then sampled regret tables, then nets that replace them. You can open the ledger at any rung and read what the solver believes, spot by spot.

What the software does

Solve. Verify. Play.

Solve

Regret matching, climbing.

The same idea at every scale: keep a ledger of how much you regret not having taken each action, and next time play in proportion to the regrets. Rock-paper-scissors needs a three-line ledger. Kuhn needs twelve. Leduc needs 288. Mini-Drawmaha needs 3.1 million spots and 1.77 GB, and full Drawmaha needs a net to hold it.

On the right, the shape of that settling: the average strategy rolls in from a corner of the triangle and circles down into the centre like a ball in a bowl, where rung 0 lands at (0.334, 0.333, 0.333) after 100,000 iterations. The exact run ticks in the console at the top of the page.

Verify

The answer key is printed on the page.

Kuhn poker has a closed-form equilibrium: the jack bluffs with probability α and the king value-bets with 3α. Rung 1 runs vanilla CFR for 100,000 iterations and lands on α = 0.220 and 0.663 for the king, a game value of −0.05555 against the exact −1/18, and 0.00063 chips per hand of exploitability.

These are the solver's own curves, straight from rung 1's export, drawn against the closed-form lines.

1 → 2

Kuhn has one card each and one round of betting. The next rung adds a board: a shared card that changes what every hand is worth, and a second betting round after it. It is the first rung where a strategy has to react to public information.
