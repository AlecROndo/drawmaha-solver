drawmaha.app · the solver's interface · design studies · 2026-10-07

Ten ways to look at a range, and a lead-up that is not a rail.

The question every view has to answer is the one you ask at the table: here is the spot; here is everything I could be holding; what does the solver do with each of them, and what does it do with mine? Every study below is drawn over the same spot and the same range, so the differences are differences of form, not of data. The leaf of every study is the same: one hand, its two halves named, its mix.

What is real and what is not. The range composition is real: 200,000 five-card holdings sampled from the 49 unseen cards on this flop, each scored twice (the inner hand from the five hole cards; the outer hand from exactly two hole cards and the three board cards), with draw flags. The action mix is illustrative: rung 4 is not trained, so a smooth function of each hand's two showdown-now equities stands in for the policy. Shapes, counts and the dimensionality are honest; the percentages are not solver output.

Part one · the lead-up

The hand so far is a scorecard, not a railway.

The station rail treats every event as the same kind of thing: a box on a line with "P0" above and "pot 2" below. But a hand of Drawmaha has two players doing things in turn across streets that have cards, and the draw is an event with a size and a face-up card. A scorecard already knows this shape: one lane per seat, one column per street, the board along the top, the pot along the bottom. You read it the way you read a box score. Three forms below, strongest first.

Lead-up A · the two-lane scoreA

Click any cell to rewind to the decision it records. The board row holds the cards; a draw column holds how many each seat threw and the face-up card when there was one. The cursor is the only yellow thing on the sheet.

Why a score beats a rail

The rail flattens two players into one sequence and makes you read "P0 · check · P1 · check" to find out who did what. Lanes answer that without reading: your lane is the bottom one, always.

Streets become columns, so the board cards sit where the street is, and the draw gets a real column with a real payload (counts, the face-up card, kept or rejected) instead of a station labelled "draw".

What it costs

It is wider than the rail for long lines (bet, raise, re-raise in one street stacks chips inside one cell). A cell can hold two chips; past that, the cell scrolls or abbreviates.

Build this. It is the lead-up for every range study below; the one-line form (B) is its collapsed state for the window title bar.
Lead-up B · the line, as notationB

The same hand as one line of poker notation with the board inline. Hovering a pill shows the pot after it; clicking rewinds. This is what the score collapses to when the range view needs the whole window.

When to use it

The range grid wants the whole window. The line fits in 40 px above it and still carries everything: who did what, the cards, the draw sizes, where you are.

Where it fails

Nobody can rewind a 14-pill line with confidence; past about eight events it should expand back into the score on hover.

Build as the collapsed form of A, not as its own thing.
Part two · ten range views

Ten forms for "everything I could have here".

Each is drawn from the same 200,000 holdings on K♠ 9♠ 4♦, facing a pot bet. The first five are maps of the range (where the mass is, what each region does); the next three are paths to a hand (ways to narrow 1.9 million holdings to one); the last two put the spot in context (the other seat, the hand's history). The notes beside each say how it answers "what do I do?", how it narrows to a hand, and what it cannot show.

The idea

Drawmaha's hold'em grid. Rows are what the five cards in your hand make on their own; columns are what two of them make with the board. A cell is one region of the range: its area is how much of the range lives there, its fill is what that region does (fold grey, call yellow, pot pink), the margins carry each row's and column's own mix.

What do I do?

Read the row you are in, then the column; the cell's bands are your answer and the neighbours say how robust it is. The margins answer the coarser question ("what does a pair in hand do here, regardless of the board?") for free.

Narrowing to a hand

Click a cell → the hands in it, sorted by share, in the drill strip below; type a hand → its cell pins and the drill lists its suit-variants.

Cannot show

Order inside a cell (which two-pair hands call and which fold) and anything about the opponent. On a flop with no straight or flush possible the made-hand columns collapse to four, so the columns must be made-hand × draw composites or the grid is empty on the right.

The home view. Everything else is a lens on it or a path out of it.
02 · the strength ribbonby total equityby innerby outerby scoop02

The idea

Throw away both category axes. Sort every holding by one number (total equity by default; the inner half, the outer half, or the scoop chance on the tabs) and lay them along a strip, weakest left, strongest right. Each sliver is one slice of the range; its stack is the mix. Polarisation is a shape: raises at both ends, calls in the middle.

What do I do?

Find your percentile (the hand field drops a marker on the strip) and read up. The three small strips underneath re-sort the same hands by inner, outer and scoop, so you see which half is making the solver act.

Narrowing to a hand

Brush a span of the strip → the hands in that span, as a list. Fast for "what are the worst hands that still call?".

Cannot show

Where the mass is by hand type: a 4-flush in hand and a pair with a flush draw can sit side by side and look alike. Needs the grid for names.

Build as the grid's second tab. Same window, same filters, one toggle.
03 · the two equities, as a plane03

The idea

A split-pot hand has two strengths, so plot them: across is the outer equity, up is the inner equity. Every holding is a point; density is drawn as cells so 1.9 million do not become soup. The corners have names: scoop (top right), Omaha only (bottom right), hand only (top left), air (bottom left). Colour is the mix.

What do I do?

Your hand is a dot you can see in relation to the whole cloud. The raise region should be a shape you can describe ("top right, plus a tongue along the bottom edge for the flush draws"), which is the kind of thing a person can remember.

Narrowing to a hand

Lasso a region → its hands. The weakest form here: a region of the plane is not a thing you can name, so you cannot come back to it.

Cannot show

Categories. Two hands at the same point can be different things; equity is a summary and this view trusts it completely.

Analysis chapter, not the UI. One figure in the paper, maybe a third tab; never the home.
04 · the ledger — hairline rows that open04
all 1.9 M›pair in hand · 42%›pair + flush draw with the board›…

The idea

The site's own form for anything tabular: hairline rows. Each row is a region of the range with its share and its mix bar; a row opens into the rows beneath it (inner class → outer class → draw flags → suit patterns → the hand). The breadcrumb is the filter you have built by opening rows. Nothing is hidden behind a hover: every number is printed.

What do I do?

Open rows until the row is your hand; every row on the way down tells you how your answer differs from the class above it, which is the explanation of the answer.

Narrowing to a hand

This is narrowing; the view and the path are the same object. Best of the ten at reaching one hand with its context intact.

Cannot show

Where the mass is at a glance; a 50% row and a 0.2% row take the same height. The marimekko variant (row height ∝ share) fixes that and makes the rare rows unreadable; a toggle, not a default.

Build as the drill. Clicking a grid cell opens the ledger at that row.
05 · one pane per action05

The idea

The homepage explorer's move: never mix three colours in one cell. Draw the same inner × outer map three times, each shaded by one action's frequency, and list each pane's heaviest hands beneath it. You compare panes instead of decoding bands.

What do I do?

Weaker at that question (your cell is in three places) but much stronger at its cousin: "which hands raise here?" is one pane, read directly.

Narrowing to a hand

The top-hands lists under each pane are a free drill: the pot pane's list is literally the raising range by example.

Cannot show

Mixes. A 50/50 cell looks like two half-shaded cells in two panes, which is correct and unmemorable.

Build as a shading mode of the grid ("shade by: mix / fold / call / pot"), not as three windows.

Every card you place removes a dimension; the header's mix re-aggregates over what is left. Five cards placed is a hand; zero is the range.

The idea

Instead of naming your hand, build it: the 49 live cards as a deck, the board cards already up. Tap cards; the range narrows live from 1.9 million to one. Each card wears a small bar for how much the current action wants it (here: how over-represented it is among raises), so the deck is also a map of what the solver likes.

What do I do?

After five taps, exactly. After two or three, honestly: "with A♠ A♦ and any three others" is a real region of the range, and the header's mix is its real answer.

Narrowing to a hand

The best path of the ten for someone holding real cards at a real table: no typing, no categories, same gesture as picking up the hand.

Cannot show

Structure. Card-level frequency is a weak signal (a K is good for everyone); the deck says little about why. Pairs with the grid, which lights the cells still alive as cards are placed.

Build as the hand input. Replaces the text field in every other study.
07 · the draw board — the action is a throw07

The idea

At the draw the solver's action is not fold / call / pot, it is one of 32 subsets of your five cards. No fold-call-pot colouring survives that. So the draw gets its own board: rows are inner classes, columns are how many you throw, the cell is how often. Under it, your hand with a throw-probability under each card, which is the only honest per-hand answer.

What do I do?

Read your row for how many; read your hand's five bars for which. The two-pair row answering "stand pat 45% · throw one 55%" is a real Drawmaha lesson (the outer half wants the fifth card).

Narrowing to a hand

Same deck input as 06; the per-card bars are the single-hand solve.

Cannot show

Which card in the "throw one" cell. That lives only at the hand level, which is why the hand strip is part of this view and not a drill.

Build; it is not optional. Every other study goes blank at the draw node without it.
08 · the funnel — filters you can see biting08

The idea

Make the filters the view. Five stages left to right: the whole range, the inner filter, the outer filter, the draw filter, the hand. Each stage shows what is left (count and width) and what it does (mix). The change in the mix between stages is the explanation: "adding the flush draw moved raise from 14% to 48%" is printed, not inferred.

What do I do?

The last stage is your hand. The four to its left are why.

Narrowing to a hand

Strongest of the ten at teaching the narrowing; weakest at doing it fast (five choices before an answer).

Cannot show

The range's shape. You see one path through it, never the whole.

Trainer mode. Not the explorer: too slow for a lookup, exactly right for a lesson.
09 · both seats — the range you are facing09

The idea

The rung-2 page's best instinct, scaled up: your map and theirs, side by side. Theirs is already filtered by what they did (this is the betting range of seat 0). Hover a cell on your side and the middle column prints your equity against their whole range, split into the two halves and the scoop chance.

What do I do?

Same as 01 on your side, plus the reason: the equity readout is the number the policy is responding to.

Narrowing to a hand

As 01. The middle column follows the hover down to a single hand.

Cannot show

Two maps halve the resolution of each; on a laptop this is two 9×8 grids of 44 px cells. Fine for reading, cramped for drilling.

Build as a toggle on 01 ("show their range"), with the equity readout appearing in the drill strip.
10 · the filmstrip — the lead-up as ranges10

One frame per event. Each frame is the acting seat's range after the event, as a thumbnail of the two-half grid, with how much of the range survived. The frame you are in is the one with the yellow border; clicking any frame rewinds and opens it as the full grid.

The idea

The lead-up and the range view are the same thing seen at two sizes. Every action in the hand was a range narrowing; show the lead-up as that: a strip of thumbnails, one per event, each the actor's range after it. The hand so far becomes a story of two ranges shrinking.

What do I do?

Not directly; this is context. But the frame before yours is their betting range, and the frame after is yours, so the story reads as "they bet with this, so you call with this".

Narrowing to a hand

None here; a frame opens as 01.

Cannot show

Draw frames: a range after a draw is a range over (hole, discard) pairs, which the thumbnail cannot draw; here the draw frames show the throw-count distribution instead.

Build later, as the score's expanded state. Hover a cell in the two-lane score and this is its popover.

The recommendation, in build order

  1. The two-lane score (A) replaces the station rail, with the one-line form (B) as its collapsed state in the window's title bar.
  2. The two-half grid (01) is the window. Its columns are made-hand × draw composites, because on most flops the made-hand axis alone is four columns wide. Cell area is share; cell fill is the mix; margins carry the marginal mixes. A shading-mode control gives study 05 for free.
  3. The ledger (04) opens under the grid when a cell is clicked and is the only way down to a single hand with its context; the breadcrumb is the filter state.
  4. The deck (06) is the hand input everywhere, replacing text entry; the grid lights the cells still alive as cards are placed.
  5. The draw board (07) replaces the grid when the spot is a draw node. Nothing else survives that node.
  6. The ribbon (02) as the grid's second tab, and both seats (09) as a toggle that adds the equity readout to the drill. The filmstrip (10) and funnel (08) wait for the trainer.
