# Completeness audit: gaps the paper must cover or scope out

1. Extensive-form game formalism and Kuhn's theorem (1953): no lane defines information sets, perfect recall, or behavioral-vs-mixed strategies, yet CFR's correctness and the abstraction lane's imperfect-recall caveat both rest on Kuhn's equivalence theorem. Also missing: the minimax theorem and equilibrium interchangeability in two-player zero-sum games — the reason ANY Nash strategy is safe heads-up, and precisely what breaks 3+ handed. A short prerequisites section (or a pointer to Shoham & Leyton-Brown / the Lanctot tutorial) is needed.

2. Exact solving via sequence-form LP (Koller, Megiddo & von Stengel 1994/1996; Gambit software): the engineering lane uses 'Leduc's sequence-form-LP first-player game value -0.0856' as a regression-test constant, but no lane explains what sequence-form LP is or that small games can be solved exactly this way. It is both the historical pre-CFR baseline and the practical tool for generating ground-truth values in the Kuhn/Leduc validation harness.

3. Pinning the exact Drawmaha rule variant and computing the real game-tree profile: Drawmaha has multiple sub-variants (draw timing relative to the flop, number of betting rounds, cards-speak vs declare, odd-chip rules, pot-limit vs limit). Several lane claims silently depend on unpinned rules — the search lane's 'one draw means only one street boundary' is false if the game has flop/turn/river betting rounds like standard Drawmaha, and the deal-space arithmetic differs. The survey needs a rules-specification section (SwC/mixed-game rules as source) with an explicit node-count/street-structure table.

4. Regret-based pruning and dynamic thresholding (Brown & Sandholm, NeurIPS 2015; AAAI 2017): the standard technique for skipping negative-regret subtrees, used in the Libratus/Pluribus blueprints and a major practical speedup for both tabular and sampled CFR. Absent from every lane despite compute budget being the survey's central constraint.

5. Modern tabular state of the art beyond DCFR: predictive/optimistic CFR+ (Farina, Kroer & Sandholm 2021, 'Faster Game Solving via Predictive Blackwell Approachability') and the first-order-methods line (Excessive Gap Technique, Hoda/Gilpin et al.) with their O(1/T) rates. The cfr-foundations lane stops at 2019, and the neural lane's Deep (Predictive) Discounted CFR (2025) presumes PCFR+ context the survey never gives.

6. Public chance sampling / vector-form CFR (Johanson et al., AAMAS 2012): traversing the public tree while vectorizing over all private hands is the trick behind PioSolver-class speed and behind the vectorized best-response pass the evaluation lane already invokes — directly relevant to handling Drawmaha's board-card chance nodes, and uncovered.

7. Hand isomorphism indexing algorithms (Waugh 2013, 'A Fast and Optimal Hand Isomorphism Algorithm'): the abstraction lane brute-force-verified 134,459 canonical 5-card classes but no lane gives the standard perfect-indexing algorithm needed to key tables, buffers, and evaluators — nor the caveat that suit symmetry shrinks once board cards and kept/discarded cards fix suits, so the 19.3x factor does not survive to later streets.

8. Discard action-space parameterization for the network: the neural lane counts the 32 keep/discard subsets but never addresses whether the net should output a flat 32-way head vs factorized per-card keep/discard logits, how regret matching behaves over combinatorial/factorized action spaces, and action masking. This is a genuine open encoding decision with no citation anywhere in the coverage.

9. Non-card state encoding design space: only card embeddings were detailed. Missing: betting-history representation (Deep CFR's bet-fraction + occurred-flag branch vs sequence models), pot/stack normalization, position, and how to encode both players' public draw-count histories into the input — the features the variant lane says must be in the infoset key need a corresponding network-input treatment.

10. Value-net dataset generation for the search phase: the search lane recommends depth-limited resolving as phase 2 but no lane covers how counterfactual-value training data is actually produced — DeepStack's random-range sampling of subgames, recursive bootstrapping from deeper solves, zero-sum consistency losses, and (for Drawmaha) emitting BOTH pot-halves' values per hand.

11. Belief/range updates across the draw for any resolving layer: updating the opponent's range through a hidden discard event requires marginalizing over which cards were kept vs mucked given only the public draw count — a draw-game-specific belief-update computation (and range-dimensionality blow-up) that no lane writes down; it is the core of the 'factorized belief representation' problem the search lane names but does not solve.

12. Deployment post-processing of strategies: purification and thresholding (Ganzfried & Sandholm 2012) — rounding near-zero action probabilities before serving reduces abstraction-noise exploitability and is standard practice for a deployed policy/dashboard; also test-time action sampling and seeding. Nothing in any lane covers what happens between 'trained average-policy net' and 'strategy shown/played'.

13. Warm-starting CFR from an existing strategy (Brown & Sandholm 2016): relevant both to the tabular-to-neural pipeline (initializing from the shrunken-variant solution) and to nested resolving; uncovered.

14. Opponent modeling and safe exploitation (Restricted Nash Response, Johanson et al. 2008; Safe Opponent Exploitation, Ganzfried & Sandholm): the survey covers only equilibrium play. Even if out of scope, it needs a scope statement — a dashboard used against real opponents immediately raises the exploit-vs-safety tradeoff, and readers will ask.

15. Distributed/parallel training engineering: multi-worker MCCFR data generation vs a central learner, GPU batching of traversals, and wall-clock throughput estimation for the C(47,k)-replacement x 32-discard draw branching. The engineering lane gives single-run hyperparameters and a compute table but no scaling-out guidance (DREAM/PokerRL's distributed setups exist as reference points).

16. Split-pot measurement conventions: the evaluation lane's calibration anchors (750 mbb/g always-fold, ~50 mbb/g pro edge) are hold'em-and-blind-structure specific and do not transfer; the survey needs Drawmaha-native units, per-half EV decomposition and scoop-rate diagnostics, and the odd-chip/both-halves-tie terminal-utility table the variant lane waves at but never specifies.

17. Actor-critic-to-regret bridges for completeness of the neural lane: NeuRD (Hennes et al. 2020), ARMAC (Gruslys et al. 2020), and QPG/RPG (Srinivasan et al. 2018) connect policy gradients to CFR and are the missing middle between the rl-equilibrium and neural-cfr lanes; optional but a survey titled 'how ML poker solvers are trained' will be dinged for omitting them.

18. Multiway play caveat: Drawmaha is typically dealt multiway in mixed games, and every guarantee in the coverage is heads-up-only. Beyond citing Pluribus, no lane states what specifically breaks with 3+ players (no unique value, Nash not safe, equilibrium computation intractable) or what the practical stance is (train HU, accept heuristic multiway play).

19. Benchmarking a first-of-its-kind agent: since no external Drawmaha bot exists (absence finding), head-to-head baselines must be homegrown — the survey should prescribe a bake-off protocol between independently-seeded/architecture-varied training runs plus the rule-based baselines, and note that real-money deployment on sites like SwC violates ToS (a one-line responsible-use note). Related small gap: rake changes the equilibrium (GTO Wizard trains across rake) — state whether the solver targets raked or rake-free play.


## Auditor notes

Contradictions and tensions to reconcile before assembly. (1) Deal-space arithmetic: cfr-foundations quotes C(52,5)^2 ~ 6.7e12 for the two-player deal space, but hands are disjoint — the correct count is C(52,5) x C(47,5) ~ 4.0e12; a survey table repeating the squared figure is wrong by ~70%. (2) Range dimensionality: search-value-nets says Drawmaha ranges are 2,598,960-dim while abstraction-bucketing says lossless isomorphism collapses hands to 134,459 classes; both are true in different contexts (suit symmetry is valid pre-deal but breaks as board/draw cards fix suits), and the survey must say so explicitly or the numbers read as conflicting. (3) Street-boundary count: search-value-nets claims 'one draw means only one street boundary needs a value function,' but variant-games says the draw 'adds streets' and standard Drawmaha has flop/turn/river betting — street-by-street solving would need a value function at each boundary; this hinges on the unpinned rule variant (missing topic 3). (4) Scoped-claim collision: cfr-foundations calls VR-MCCFR 'the only known way to combine CFR+-style updates with sampling' while neural-cfr calls Deep (Predictive) Discounted CFR 'the first credible bridge' for discounted updates in neural CFR — not strictly contradictory (tabular-sampled vs neural), but unscoped they will read as mutually exclusive firsts. (5) Split-pot difficulty: variant-games calls split pot 'the cheapest of the three problems' (engine view: terminal utilities only) while abstraction-bucketing says split pot makes strength two-dimensional and degrades scalar abstraction (representation view) — compatible, but the survey should state the reconciliation (cheap for the game engine, expensive for features/abstraction) rather than leave both claims bare. (6) Internal date mismatches: neural-cfr lists Deep (Predictive) Discounted CFR as 2025 in its paper list but 'AAAI 2026' in notes; rl-equilibrium lists MMD as 2022 (arXiv) but 'ICLR 2023' in notes — pick one citation convention (venue year vs preprint year) survey-wide. (7) Minor unit hazard: AlphaHoldem's 111.56 mbb/h vs GTO Wizard's 19.4bb/100 vs Cepheus's mbb/g — three unit spellings appear across lanes; the evaluation lane's conversion (bb/100 = mbb/g / 10) should be applied uniformly and 'mbb/h' vs 'mbb/g' normalized. Overall assessment: the eight lanes cover the algorithmic spine (CFR -> MCCFR -> Deep CFR), search, RL alternatives, abstraction, evaluation, variant precedent, and tooling well; the systematic holes are (a) assumed-but-never-taught game-theory prerequisites, (b) the exact-solving/LP and pruning/predictive-CFR corners of the tabular literature, (c) everything between trained network and deployed dashboard (post-processing, belief tracking through draws, serving), and (d) Drawmaha rule pinning plus split-pot measurement conventions, on which several existing lane claims quietly depend. No file outputs were produced; this analysis is self-contained.
